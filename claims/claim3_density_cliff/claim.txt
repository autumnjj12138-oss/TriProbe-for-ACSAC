CLAIM 3 -- Mask density has a sharp cliff, and the hard cap keeps the system
           below it.
Paper: Section 6.3 and the mask-density-drift analysis.

WHAT THE PAPER CLAIMS
Sparse federated training with symmetric prune-and-recover lets mask density
drift upward until consensus fusion loses its pruning margin, which is why the
Lockdown baseline collapses in claim 1. TriProbe uses asymmetric rates
(alpha_prune = 0.05 > alpha_recover = 0.01), whose fixed point is
alpha_r/(alpha_p+alpha_r) = 0.167. That value sits directly on a cliff: the
defense is healthy at any cap up to 0.16 and collapses completely at 0.18. The
hard cap exists to hold density below the cliff rather than at its edge.

HOW IT IS CHECKED
Every cap at or below 0.16 must give ASR under 5%; every cap at or above 0.18
must exceed 95%. A single 0.02 step spans the entire range, so no numerical
difference between machines can blur it.

This is the cheapest and most decisive claim to verify, and the best one to run
first: the two regimes differ by two orders of magnitude in ASR and 16 points of
benign accuracy.
