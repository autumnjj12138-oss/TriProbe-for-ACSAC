CLAIM 2 -- Two of TriProbe's four components do the work.
Paper: Section 6.3, Table 5.

WHAT THE PAPER CLAIMS
TriProbe combines triggered-probe filtering (ASF), a hard cap on client mask
density, Layer-1 cross-subspace blocking, and end-of-training consensus fusion.
The first two are decisive; the other two are auxiliary. Removing either core
mechanism destroys the defense. Removing either auxiliary one changes ASR by
well under a percentage point.

This claim was strengthened in response to review: the submitted paper presented
all four components as comparable contributors, which the five-seed ablation
does not support.

REFERENCE VALUES (5 seeds, GTX 1650)
  full TriProbe            0.86 +/- 0.68 %
  w/o triggered-probe ASF  45.62 +/- 6.06 %     <- decisive
  w/o hard density cap    100.00 +/- 0.00 %     <- decisive
  w/o Layer-1 hard block    1.06 +/- 0.74 %     <- auxiliary, +0.20 pp
  w/o final consensus fusion 0.90 +/- 0.68 %    <- auxiliary, +0.04 pp

HOW IT IS CHECKED
Removing ASF must raise ASR by more than 20x; removing the density cap must
exceed 50%; each auxiliary component must move ASR by less than 2 pp. The gap
between the two groups is more than two orders of magnitude, so the ranking
reproduces even where absolute values shift.
